# Walkthrough and user guide

This guide mirrors the Help in the app. Help opens from Settings, Help, "Help and walkthrough" (the topic list);
from the "Take the tour" card on the home screen; from the "Take the tour" button on the last first-run step (it
works before the first unlock); and in a session from the officer's three-dot menu, Help, or More options, Session,
Help, both of which open "During a session". The topics are: First walkthrough, During a session, The conversation,
Every setting, Records and reports, Practice, Station computer, and Privacy and safety. The topic list has a "Search
help" field that matches titles, summaries and entry text. First walkthrough is shown one step at a time ("Step 1 of
8") with Back, Next and Done. Help is available in English and Hindi.

Sign Setu helps an officer talk with a person who is deaf or hard of hearing, a young child, or someone who speaks
another language. It is an aid, not an interpreter. The phone works with no internet, and the record stays on the
phone unless an online service is switched on.

## 1. First walkthrough

1. **What Sign Setu is.** An aid for an officer talking with a person who is deaf or hard of hearing, a young child,
   or someone who speaks another language. It works with no internet; the record stays on the phone unless an
   online service is switched on.
2. **Start a session.** On the home screen answer "Who are you helping?": pick their role (or leave "Not known
   yet") and their profile: young child, signs, other language or not sure. Choose "Their language", or "Do not
   know · find their language" to let them choose on the next screen. Tap Start session.
3. **The stage faces the person.** The stage is a video call: the character, a caption in their language and their
   own camera window. At first only the character and caption show; one tap shows every button for 5 seconds. The
   Stop octagon is always in the corner.
4. **Take the phone back.** Hold the small officer handle in the top corner of the stage for a third of a second,
   until its ring closes. Back does nothing on the stage, and the app stays pinned so the person cannot leave it.
5. **Ask something.** On the officer page tap a quick question, or type in "Type anything to show them", then tap
   Show to them. The character signs, gestures, spells or holds up pictures, as set by the Signs, Gestures, Spell
   and Pictures toggles.
6. **Get the answer.** The person answers with Yes, No, pictures, drawing, writing, or by signing to the camera. The
   answer appears in the conversation with how it was given. A reading made by the app is marked not checked; only
   the person's own Yes confirms a meaning.
7. **If they need a break.** If the person taps Stop, a calm breathing screen covers the stage and the officer sees
   a banner to pause and check they are comfortable. Only their "I'm ready" ends the pause.
8. **Finish and save.** Finish session is at the foot of More options and in the menu. Name the session, then tap
   Save and finish or Export report. The report password is shown only once. Help is always in Settings and in
   the session menu.

## 2. During a session

### Two sides, one phone

The phone is either on the stage, facing the person, or on the officer page, held by the officer. The stage never
shows notes, the log, or anything typed but not sent.

### The stage buttons

- Tap anywhere to show the buttons; they fade 5 seconds after the last touch. With a screen reader on, the buttons
  stay.
- Left: replay and signing speed (½×, ¾×, 1×).
- Right: the person's camera window. Drag it, or tap to enlarge. During a video call, tapping the small
  picture-in-picture swaps camera and signer; the choice survives rotation.
- Below: Next, Yes, No, the honest answers (I don't know, I don't understand, That's wrong), Draw and Write when
  offered, and Water, Food and Help.
- The skeleton badge works as an on/off switch.
- The officer handle (hold it for a third of a second) returns the phone to the officer page. A screen reader user
  can use its long-press action.
- System Back closes the express board; everywhere else on the stage it does nothing.
- With "They sit opposite me" the stage turns 180 degrees, and the express board turns with it.

### The officer page

The top bar shows the profile, language and role, with Call (interpreter), the session log and the menu. Below are
the conversation, quick questions, the Ready to show strip and the composer at the foot (the dock). The menu
button is labelled More options.

### Asking a question

Tap a quick question to prepare it in Ready to show, check its marks for sign, gesture, audio and caption, then tap
Show to them. "Ask next" suggests the next welfare question. Long-press quick questions to queue them with Add to
run, or tap Safety basics: up to five go in one handover.

### More options

The More page holds every question group and phrase (Personal, Family, Address, School and others), They gestured,
the picture board, Draw, the language finder, Tell me, the ISL dictionary, the session log with notes, Second
screen, and the profile, role and language. Picking something returns to the conversation. Its "Other ways to ask"
row has They gestured, Pictures, Draw and Language. Its "Session" list: "Let the AI counsellor talk for you" (when
the counsellor is available), ISL dictionary (ASL dictionary when signing ASL), Second screen, Replay intro and
Help, which opens "During a session"; Finish session sits below them.

### The menu

The three-dot menu has Sign language (switch between ISL and ASL for the rest of the session, after a check), They
sit opposite me (turns the stage 180 degrees), Show skeleton on camera (a switch), Help (opens "During a session")
and Finish session. ASL is marked Experimental.

### Sign windows

A sign window opens for up to 20 seconds when the person raises their hands, taps the camera button, or the officer
taps Read signs. One dot appears per sign seen; the stage never shows what was read. Quiet hands for 2 seconds,
Done, or the time limit close it. The officer page shows the app's reading per sign, marked not checked. Replay at
¼× or ½×, pick one of the top four guesses per sign, or use "Ask them to choose" so the person picks a picture.
Closing a window never waits more than about 2.5 seconds for the station; if the station is slow or fails, the
phone's own reading stands.

### The express board

Help on the stage opens the full picture board grouped as Feelings, Needs, Urgent, Safety, People, Places, Answers
and Body. Every card is at least 56 dp. Water and Food are requests the person can make at any time.

### The body map

Help, Body shows a front and back figure. A touch answers as pain and records the side, the body part, the person's
own left or right and the exact point, for example: body map, front: left chest.

### Stop and the pause

Stop ends nothing by itself. It covers the stage with a night-sky pause: a breathing circle that grows for 4
seconds, holds for 2 and shrinks for 6, and bubbles to pop, with no score, sound or timer. The officer gets a banner.
Only the person's "I'm ready" ends it, and its length is logged.

### Recording their voice

The microphone in the dock opens Record their voice. Consent comes first: ask the person (for a child, the parent
or guardian), then tap "They agreed to recording"; the answer is logged. The take stops by itself when they finish,
or tap again. The voice is kept encrypted in the session record and goes in the export. With the station computer,
live words appear marked not confirmed.

### Second screen

More, Second screen lets the person see a second department phone or tablet while the officer keeps this one. Pick
a device paired in Bluetooth settings, or Find a nearby phone, type the 6 digits it shows, check that both show the
same check number, and tap Match on the other screen. A bar shows the device, its battery and the last answer. If
the link drops there are Reconnect and Use this phone. The sheet notices Bluetooth being switched on outside the
app.

### Auto

With Auto on, a card proposes the next welfare item and why. Each goes to the stage after a short gap; pause, skip
or choose something else at any time. Show and Skip apply only to the proposal on screen when tapped. Stop pauses
Auto; a second Stop ends it. Ask again on any asked question puts it back on the stage.

### Calling an interpreter

Call in the officer bar opens the messaging app set in Settings with the interpreter contact. The app unpins only
for that call, then returns. If the app fails to open, a pinned session stays pinned.

### Ending and saving

Finish session shows a summary: time taken, what was shown and answered, and anything left open. "Thank them" on the
stage closes the conversation for the person. Name the session and tap Save and finish, or Export report. Delete
without saving needs a second tap. Back never finishes a session: it returns home with the session still open, and
Continue resumes it. Ending a session stops any pending auto-mode, question-run or language timer, and nothing more
is logged.

## 3. The conversation

- **Transcript.** Every question shown appears on the right; every answer on the left, with how it was given (tap,
  sign, picture, drawing, writing, voice) and how long it took. Grades show how sure the record is: confirmed by the
  person, said by them, or read by the app and not checked.
- **Typing to them.** Type in "Type anything to show them" and tap Send. While typing, the suggestions give way to
  the Signs, Gestures, Spell and Pictures toggles. The character signs known words, fingerspells the rest and holds
  up each word's picture. The + button attaches answer pictures first.
- **Ready to show.** A prepared item waits with marks for sign, gesture, audio and caption. Tap Show to them to send
  it, or the cross to drop it.
- **Say it back.** Under any answer, Say it back lets the officer edit the reading and show it. The person sees the
  words and answers Yes or No. Only their Yes confirms the meaning; No marks it Said no.
- **Assistant.** Assistant in the dock offers a caption in the person's language (machine translation, not
  checked), what an answer may mean, and non-leading suggested follow-up questions. It is tried on the station
  first, then the phone's model, then the gateway or saved service; a station error falls through to the next.
  Nothing it writes reaches the stage until the officer shows it.
- **AI counsellor.** With the online assistant enrolled, the counsellor can hold a gentle conversation. Each line
  sends on its own after two seconds; Send now, Another line, or Take over stops it. It pauses and hands back to the
  officer if the person asks to stop, may be at risk, or the assistant does not answer (no reply within 150
  seconds), and after thirty turns. Start again resumes it; Resume or Take over are offered after a failed request.
- **Session log and notes.** The log icon opens every row with its time and kind. Notes stay on the officer page and
  in the record; they are never shown on the stage.
- **Translate app.** When the person's language has no captions, a Translate app chip opens the phone's translation
  app. Nothing from the session is passed to it.

## 4. Every setting

Settings groups, top to bottom: Help, Type and sign (practice), Interpreter call, Security, Stage, Online, Offline,
Session records, About. Defaults are in brackets.

### Help

Help and walkthrough ("Every screen, setting and call step explained") opens the topic list of the in-app Help.

### Practice (group headed Type and sign)

- **Type and sign.** Type any sentence and watch it signed word by word, outside a session. Nothing is recorded.
- **Sign to Type.** Sign to the camera and see the words build into a sentence. Shown only when the phone can read
  signs.
- **Record signs for training.** Sign 10 words and sentences to help the sign reader learn new signers. Shown only
  in development builds on phones that can read signs.

### Interpreter call

- **Messaging app.** WhatsApp or Sandes, used by the Call button (not set).
- **Interpreter contact.** Shown once a messaging app is set. For WhatsApp, the 10-digit mobile number or the full
  number with country code; for Sandes, the contact name (empty).
- **Call interpreter now.** Shown once a messaging app is set. Opens the messaging app with the saved contact, to
  test it. The same button is on the home screen and in a session.

### Security

Turning any security switch off asks for the officer's fingerprint or screen lock, so the person cannot.

- **Pin the app during a session.** The person cannot leave the app while holding the phone (on).
- **Block screenshots.** Screenshots and screen recording show a blank screen (on in the release build). Keep it on
  for real cases.
- **Ask for fingerprint.** Asks for fingerprint or screen lock before opening the app, leaving a session or deleting
  (on).

Pinning and the fingerprint prompt default to off only in unlocked test builds.

### Stage

- **Camera on the stage.** The person sees their own hands and signs can be read. Nothing is recorded or saved
  (on). Turn off only if the person is uncomfortable with a camera.
- **Gesture speed.** Shown as "Gesture speed: 1.00×". How fast the character signs, 0.25× to 1.5× in steps of 0.25
  (1.00×). Slow it for a young child or a new signer; the stage also has its own ½× and ¾×.
- **Sign in ISL order.** On: typed sentences are reordered into ISL grammar, time first and the question word last.
  Off: words are signed as typed (on).
- **3D character.** On: the 3D character. Off: the drawn character, lighter, for slower phones (on). The drawn one
  is used by itself where 3D cannot run.

### Online

- **Online assistant.** Labelled "Online assistant (department gateway)", or "Online assistant (third party)" when
  the build has no gateway. When online, sends masked text to the department gateway (or an outside AI service) for
  reports and suggestions (off). A notice explains what is sent before it turns on. The phone works the same without
  it.
- **Enrolment code.** Shown with the department gateway while the online assistant is on. Enter the code the
  department gives; it then reads Enrolled.
- **Custom key.** Points the assistant at any OpenAI-style service: Address, API key and Model, with Find models,
  Test and Remove. The key is kept encrypted on the phone and is not kept in saved screen state, so after a rotation
  a typed but unsaved key must be entered again. Shown only when the build has no gateway; with a gateway, a
  custom service shows as Custom API endpoint.
- **Station computer.** Pairs the phone with the station's local AI brain (off). Each service has its own switch.
  See section 7.

### Offline

- **On-device assistant.** An optional model that translates typed text into the person's language and back with no
  internet. Import model file installs the file the department gives; Remove the model deletes it. Needs at least 3
  GB of memory.
- **Offline sentence helper.** An optional small model that puts the likeliest checked sentence first when a signed
  answer is unclear. It never adds words. Imported and removed the same way.

### Session records

- **Delete automatically after.** Sessions older than this are deleted even if nobody ended them: 4, 8, 12, 24, 48
  or 72 hours, or Keep until deleted (24 hours). Follow the department's rule.
- **Check a report.** Open an exported PDF with its passphrase and check its seal. See section 5.
- **Delete all data.** Destroys the encryption key, so every session on the phone becomes unreadable and cannot be
  recovered. Settings are kept. Needs a second confirmation.

### About

- **Appearance.** Opens its own page. Light or dark: Device default, Light or Dark (Device default); Pure black
  (AMOLED), shown in dark mode only (off); Colour theme: Emerald (default), Mocha, Cerulean, Amethyst, High
  contrast, Teal, Tidal wave, Tokyo night, Yin yang, Yotsuba. High contrast also turns the stage black, white and
  yellow.
- **App language.** Same as the phone (default), English or Hindi, for the officer's screens. The person's language
  is chosen per session on the home screen.
- **Open-source licences and version.** Open-source licences lists the symbols, voices and libraries in the app;
  the Sign Setu row below shows the version and build date, and the notice that the app is an aid, not an
  interpreter.
- **ISL and ASL.** "Indian Sign Language (ISL)", marked Main, and "American Sign Language (ASL)", marked
  Experimental. Shows what the phone reads and signs in Indian Sign Language (the main language) and American
  Sign Language (experimental), and how well. Choose the sign language on the home screen; switch it in a session
  from the menu.

An unreadable settings file falls back to the defaults.

## 5. Records and reports

- **Saved sessions** are listed on the home screen to Open again or Delete. Long-press to choose several, then
  delete the chosen sessions. An unfinished session shows at the top with Continue and End it. Sessions are deleted
  after the time set in Settings.
- **Export report** makes an encrypted, sealed PDF of the session. The password is shown only once: give it
  separately from the file, for example by reading it out. Then Share report and Done.
- **Send or save a saved session.** An open saved session can be sent to the station computer (Send to the station)
  or written to the phone (Save to this phone). The PDF opens with the passphrase shown; give it only to whoever
  needs the report.
- **Check a report.** Settings, Check a report: open the PDF, enter its passphrase and tap Check. It says whether
  the seal is valid, whether it matches the session still on the phone, or whether the record was altered after it
  was sealed. A report with a damaged seal is reported as an invalid signature.

## 6. Practice

- **Type and sign:** Settings, Practice, Type and sign. Type any sentence and watch the character sign it word by
  word, to learn what the person will see. The word list wraps onto new lines for long sentences.
- **Sign to Type:** sign to the camera and see the words build into a sentence in English and Hindi from read words
  and held letters. A word's other guesses are a tap away.
- **Record signs for training:** sign 10 words and sentences to the camera to help the reader learn new signers.

Nothing in practice is recorded, except training signs the signer chooses to contribute.

## 7. Station computer

The station computer runs the local AI brain: speech to text, translation and sign order, AI help and an inbox for
conversations. It stays at the station; without it the phone works alone. See [18](18-station.md) and
[16](16-relay.md).

1. **Start it.** On the station computer run `start.ps1` (Windows) or `./start.sh`. It prints a ticket, a token, an
   https address and a certificate pin. `./start.sh --rotate-token` issues a new token; every phone must pair
   again.
2. **Pair the phone.** Settings, Online, Station computer: turn it on, tap Pair with the station computer and
   enter what the computer shows: "Ticket, or address (https://…:8000)", Token and, for an address, Certificate
   pin; then Pair. A pasted ticket may
   contain line breaks. The ticket and token work on the station Wi-Fi and from anywhere. The https address, token
   and pin work on the station network only (IPv6 unique-local addresses count as local). Allow access to devices
   on this network when asked.
3. **From anywhere.** The ticket reaches the computer over an end-to-end encrypted link: directly where possible,
   otherwise through the department's own relay, which forwards data it cannot read. No port is opened at the
   station. The phone uses the relay named in the ticket; if the ticket names an n0 relay, the phone dials direct
   only. The phone has no relay setting.
4. **Status and services.** The setting shows Off, Connecting, or Connected with the number of services in use. Turn
   off each service alone: Speech to text (live and saved), Sign reading (ISL), AI help (captions, sentences,
   counsellor, summary), Receiving conversations. "Forget the station
   computer" removes the pairing.

Further facts:

- The phone's link identity is created once per install, stored encrypted with a hardware-backed key, and survives
  restarts but not reinstalls or restores from backup.
- After a network change, turning the link off, or the app going to the background, the link is dropped and dialled
  again later. A slow or failed station never blocks the phone's own reading.
- With no `--relay`, the brain uses no relay and no n0 service; phones reach it by a direct path only. If the relay
  is unreachable at start the brain logs it and carries on; the ticket still names the relay. If the iroh link
  fails entirely, the brain serves the station network only.
- The default inbox for received conversations is the folder "Sign Setu" in Documents.

## 8. Privacy and safety

- **Stays on the phone.** Every session is encrypted on the phone and deleted after the retention time. The camera
  picture is never recorded or saved. Voice is kept only after consent is logged.
- **What can go online.** Nothing, unless the online assistant is switched on or a station computer is paired. The
  online assistant receives masked text only. The station computer keeps nothing except a conversation sent to its
  inbox.
- **Protecting the screen.** The app is pinned in a session, screenshots are blocked, and leaving a session or
  deleting asks for fingerprint or screen lock. Turning these off needs the officer, not the person.
- **Reports.** A report is encrypted with a one-time password and sealed with the phone's key, so any change can be
  found with Check a report. The signing key is held in StrongBox where available, otherwise in the phone's secure
  hardware.
- **An aid, not an interpreter.** Readings by the app are marked not checked. For rights and anything that matters
  in law, use a qualified interpreter; the record says shown, not understood.
